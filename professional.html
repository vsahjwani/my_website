OPN Healthcare · Jul 2026 – Present · Glendale, CA

The uncomfortable part of patient stratification is that there's no ground truth to check yourself against — you're not predicting a label that eventually gets revealed, you're arguing that a clustering is meaningful at all. That's why most of this role has been about benchmarking, not modeling: running the same patient population through MDBSCAN, GMM, LCA, and Archetypal Analysis and asking which clusters are actually stable versus which ones just look convincing in one particular run. The most useful thing I've built isn't a single model, it's a way of engineering multi-source healthcare records into a dataset stable enough that the differences between methods reflect something real about patient similarity, not just noise in how the data was assembled. It's ongoing, but it's already reshaped how skeptical I am of any clustering result I can't stress-test.

Quantitative Data Intern

Alpha Alternatives · Jul – Aug 2025 · Mumbai

Most of this internship was spent on a problem nobody puts in a textbook: the data you actually need — mutual fund portfolios, bulk deals — mostly exists as PDFs and half-structured web pages, not clean tables. I built the scraping and parsing pipelines to fix that, which taught me that data engineering and data science aren't really separate jobs, just two ends of the same problem. The part I found genuinely interesting was further downstream — using Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes and Kalman filtering to model fund flows as a mean-reverting time series instead of a random walk. It reframed what I thought a "clean dataset" even meant: the millions of rows I'd normalized were only useful once I had a model that assumed something specific about how they moved over time.

Data Science Intern

IDXExchange · Jun – Aug 2025

This was the first time I was responsible for a team's output, not just my own, and it changed how I thought about the modeling. Benchmarking Linear Regression against Random Forest against XGBoost for property price prediction is a well-worn exercise, but leading the intern team through it meant the real work was making sure everyone's piece — the feature engineering, the geospatial EDA, the eventual model — actually fit together instead of just existing in separate notebooks. The test that mattered most wasn't the standard train/test split; it was validating on months the model hadn't seen yet, a much less forgiving way to find out whether an R² is real or just memorized. Presenting the results to senior data scientists and getting sent back to iterate more than once was its own lesson in how much a model's first version is really just a draft.

Tutor, Math Academic Success Center

UC San Diego · Sep 2024 – Jun 2026 · Part-time

Tutoring turned out to be the best diagnostic I've had for whether I actually understand something, because a room full of students will find the gap in your explanation faster than any professor will. The students who struggled most were rarely missing the mechanics — they could follow the steps — what they were missing was confidence that they were allowed to get it wrong on the way to getting it right. Most sessions ended up being less about the specific problem on the page and more about getting someone to try an approach they weren't sure about yet. It's a strange thing to put next to internships on a resume, but explaining the same concept five different ways until one of them lands taught me more about communicating technical work clearly than most of the technical work itself did.

Market Research Intern

Samyog Health Foods · Jun – Sep 2022 · Mumbai

This was my first project where the data came from actual conversations instead of a downloaded dataset — I designed and ran surveys to figure out whether Prolicious could realistically expand into a new market. The uncomfortable lesson was that people are bad at predicting their own behavior; what respondents said they'd buy and what the follow-up questions revealed about their actual habits didn't always line up, and learning to trust the second one over the first was most of the analysis. It was a small project, but it's the reason I still think about the gap between stated and revealed preference before I trust any survey number at face value.
