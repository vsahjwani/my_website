Data Science Associate Present

OPN Healthcare · Jul 2026 – Present · Glendale, CA

The challenging part of patient stratification is that there's no ground truth to check the model against. The goal is to show that a cluster is meaningful without having a label to measure how apt the model is. That's why most of my role at OPN revolved around this role has been about benchmarking, not modeling: running the same patient population through MDBSCAN, GMM, LCA, and Archetypal Analysis and asking which clusters are actually stable versus which ones just look convincing in one particular run. The most useful thing I've built is a way of engineering multi-source healthcare records into a dataset stable enough that the differences between methods reflect something real about patient similarity. It's ongoing, but it's already reshaped how skeptical I am of the plethora of decisions made in data driven models.

Quantitative Data Intern

Alpha Alternatives · Jul – Aug 2025 · Mumbai

rec letter ↗

Most of this internship was spent on a problem I wish an intern could fix for me: the data that gives you a competitive edge is usually unformatted, not easy to obtain, and exists in large amounts like mutual fund portfolios and bulk deals that mostly exist as heavily nonuniform PDFs and half-structured web pages. I built the scraping and parsing pipelines to fix that, which taught me that data engineering and data science aren't separate jobs, just two ends of the same problem. The part I found genuinely interesting was further downstream, using Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes and Kalman filtering to model fund flows as a mean-reverting time series instead of a random walk. It reframed what I thought a "clean dataset" even meant: the millions of rows I'd normalized were only useful once I had a model that assumed something specific about how they moved over time. This reminded me of a quote by Steve Jobs I relate with as a Mathematics enjoyer: "If you define the problem correctly, you almost have the solution"

Data Science Intern

IDXExchange · Jun – Aug 2025

presentation ↗

This was the first time I was responsible for a team's output, and it changed how I thought about the modelling. Benchmarking Linear Regression against Random Forest against XGBoost for property price prediction is a well-worn exercise, but leading the intern team through it meant the real work was making sure everyone's piece: the feature engineering, the EDA, the eventual model actually fit together and were logically executed so that no inconsistencies overflowed into the project. During each meeting I prepared what we had to review and our takeaways to ensure that we were on track and so that we could discuss decisions regarding handling missingness, high skews, etc. I also enjoyed the experience of discussing the pros and cons of why certain models could represent our data better and how could we innovatively create something that really used our data to its full potential.

2024

Wealth Management Intern

Kredere Wealth Partners · Jul – Aug 2024 · Mumbai

This was my first real look at how much of wealth management is just disciplined reading. Every morning started the same way, Economic Times, Financial Times, Trading Economics, and turned into a digest that someone senior would use to talk to a client that day. What surprised me was how little room there was for a hot take; a mutual fund report only mattered if I could show my reasoning held up against the last six months of data, not just the last headline. Working alongside my mentor on Indian mutual fund research taught me the difference between summarizing a market and having a position on it, and how much more careful you have to be once your writing might shape someone else's portfolio.

2022

Market Research Intern

Samyog Health Foods · Jun – Sep 2022 · Mumbai

This was my first project where the data came from conversations with real customers instead of a downloaded dataset; I designed and ran surveys to figure out whether Prolicious could realistically expand into a new market. The uncomfortable lesson was that people are bad at predicting their own behavior; what respondents said they'd buy and what the follow-up questions revealed about their actual habits didn't always line up, and learning to trust the second one over the first was most of the analysis. It was a small project, but it's the reason I still think about the gap between stated and revealed preference before I trust any survey number at face value.
