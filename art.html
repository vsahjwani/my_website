Art

As an introspective person, most of these pieces started from personal experience: questions about who I am, where I'm from, and where I might end up. Tap any image to see every photo of that piece.

A Healthy Meal
Oil on canvas · 91.44 × 91.44 cm
Inspired by Thukral & Tagra's surrealism and Carol Ann Duffy's poem sitting with the contrast between a voiceless animal's dreams and how easily meat gets consumed today. The green gumball at the centre stands for our never-ending wants.
Profile Picture
Oil on canvas, round format
On the theme of identity: to be yourself means doing what feels right, not what's expected of you. The circular canvas and centred face echo a social-media profile photo, the one image people pick to show the world who they are, and end up living up to. Black sunglasses bring a contemporary edge to otherwise sombre colours. The reference was a photo of my sister; I spent hours on the room reflected in her eyes and the creases of her lips.
Awash
3 pieces, prints of pen-&-ink drawings on paper + acrylic on glass · 37.4 × 32.3 cm each
A study of a flush, moving with its formations and its swift, forceful movement. Detailed black-and-white pattern work contrasts the bold, unpredictable colour of the paint, holding both the structure and the chaos of the same instant.
Folk Flowers for Folk
Installation of paper flower prints in poster paint & watercolour, glass mirror, wooden frame · 110 × 125 cm
Drawing on folk-art traditions from across cultures including Tanjore, Ukiyo-e, Australian, Nianhu, Mexican, Warli, Gond, Hungarian, to show how universal flowers are used as a gift. Layered circular blooms hang overhead like a garden, doubled by a mirror below.
Resurface
Graphite & charcoal
A tiger breaking the surface of the water, held there in fine tonal contrast, graphite for the soft mist, charcoal for the stripes.
Where's Vani
Watercolour, ink & colour pencil on cartridge paper · 65 × 46 cm
Resonating with Waldo, when I made this piece I didn't know where I'd study, so I hid a smaller version of myself, sunglasses on and always running, in each of the three places I could have moved to: Singapore, India, and the United States, each drawn in a style true to its own culture.
Ever Forward
Watercolour on cartridge paper · 60 × 48 cm
Dreaming ahead without forgetting my roots. I painted myself by the Corinthian pillars I loved most about my school building, looking out into colourful uncertainty. Inspired by realist painter Shuchi Krishan.
